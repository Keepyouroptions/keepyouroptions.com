# Option Definition and Keep Your Option Blog Name

The word option traces back to the Latin *optio*, meaning choice or selection. At its core, an option is one path picked out of several available ones — the freedom to choose between alternatives, or a feature you can add on top of something else, the way a car buyer picks upgrades from a trim list.

The catch is that "option" doesn't mean quite the same thing in every context. Here's how it breaks down.

## 1. Finance and Investing

In the markets, an option is a derivative contract. It hands the buyer the right to buy or sell an underlying asset at a set price within a fixed window — and critically, the buyer is never obligated to follow through.

- **Call option** — the right to buy a stock at a set price.
- **Put option** — the right to sell a stock at a set price.

Traders lean on options for two main reasons: hedging existing portfolio risk, or adding leverage to a market position.

## 2. Contractual and Legal

Outside of trading, an option can be a legal agreement that grants someone a specific right for a defined period — the right to lease a property, buy it, or renew a contract down the line. A homebuyer might pay for an option to purchase a house before committing to a mortgage. A company might grant stock options to an employee as part of a compensation package, letting them buy shares later at today's price.

## 3. Education

In school, an option usually means an elective — a course a student picks voluntarily rather than one required by the core curriculum.

## 4. Common Synonyms

People often swap "option" for "choice," but there's a subtle difference: an option implies a right that's been granted or guaranteed, not just a preference floating in the abstract. Alternative, selection, and pick land in similar territory, though none carry quite the same weight of guaranteed access.

## Why This Blog Is Called Keep Your Options

This blog leans almost entirely on the first definition — options in finance and investing. It's a running record of what I'm learning about trading and using options, written as I go rather than after the fact.
