I'd read about options in finance for years, through school, then through my career, but I never paid them much attention. They seemed too risky, too far out of reach for an ordinary individual investor, more a hedging tool for professionals than something I'd ever touch. Over the last few years that changed. I kept coming across real stories of people using options well, with real knowledge behind them, and getting real results. So I started my own path: reading, learning, trying things out.

I'm also an avid gardener, and I spend a lot of my free time out there. Last year my garlic chive plants had an incredible harvest. I decided to make garlic chive flower sauce, which meant picking every flower bud before it opened (that's when they're best). One Saturday in August, I went out and harvested the whole crop. The sauce turned out wonderful. I stored jars of it in the fridge and felt pretty proud of myself. I did feel a twinge of guilt, though. The honey bees show up every year when the chives bloom, and this year there'd be nothing for them.

A week later, something remarkable happened. Every one of those plants had put out a fresh round of flower buds, as full and abundant as before. It was as if I'd never touched them.

That's the thing about nature. It doesn't quit. Those plants threw everything they had into producing *options*: more buds, more chances to flower, more paths to reproduce. That's where our name came from. **Keep Your Options Open.**
